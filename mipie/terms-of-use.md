## 3. How the app works

The app records **only some** of the Wi-Fi networks you connect to. The goal is to keep only the places where you **settled in to work**.

**Recorded**

- You connected to a network that required a password or a login, and
- You were **not in motion** at that moment, and
- **You had not recently been in a vehicle**, and
- The network was **not a personal hotspot**, and
- **Your location could be determined accurately enough**, and
- That network name is one the app has not recorded before

Cafés, libraries, and community centers typically fall into this category.

**Not recorded**

- Open networks that require no password (for example `SEOUL`, `PublicWiFi@…`, subway networks)
- **While you are in motion** — buses and other places you merely pass through
- **When you have been in a vehicle within the last few minutes** — including a bus stopped at a light
- **Personal hotspots** — a hotspot follows a person, not a place
- **Where location accuracy is poor** — subways and other underground spaces
- **Networks using outdated security** (WEP)
- Networks already recorded (home, workplace, and so on). A trailing band or floor marker (`Cafe` and `Cafe_5G`) is treated as **the same place**

**An existing record can also be removed.** If the same access point is seen again more than 500 m from where it was recorded, the app treats that Wi-Fi as something that moves — a bus or a train — rather than a fixed place, and deletes the record automatically. The app tells you when this happens.

Motion is judged from **the travel speed in your location data together with your device's recent activity history.** This is **an estimate and is not perfect**, so an unwanted record may still be created. **You can delete any unwanted record by tapping it on the map.**

Reading activity history requires **Motion & Fitness permission**; if you deny it, no records are created.

Pins on the map **look the same regardless of the kind of network.** You can give a record your own name; if you do not, the network name is used.

Because of iOS restrictions, **records are created only while the app is on screen.** If you do not open the app, connecting to a network produces no record. This is not a defect in the app — iOS does not notify apps of Wi-Fi connections in the background.

## 4. Notice about location accuracy

Recorded locations are **estimates derived from GPS, Wi-Fi, and cellular signals, and they contain error.**

**The 50-meter circle shown on the map is an approximation, not an exact boundary.** The point where you actually connected may lie outside the circle. Records from this app must not be used as proof of location or for any comparable purpose.
